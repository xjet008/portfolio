// story


        

          I started in 2019 with Bitcoin Cash (BCH) because I was nosy.
        


        

          I'm not a trader. Please do not send me charts.
          They put me to sleep. I would rather scroll on X,
          spam emojis in Discord, break a Linux box,
          or reread a document I will lose by morning.
        


        

          I mined Monero, Cardano, and a few other coins to see
          how blocks actually get made — and why the machine
          sounded like it was preparing for takeoff.
        


        

          That curiosity turned into nodes.
          Verifier nodes. Validator nodes. Testnets.
          Whatever I could get my hands on.
        


        

          Old PCs, laptops, dedicated boxes, servers,
          shared hardware — if it booted Linux, it got a job.
        


        

          If it didn't...
        


        

          I yelled at it.
        


        

          Sometimes I spam gib wl gib wl into Discord
          when I'm trying to secure an NFT GTD or whitelist.
        


        

          Not because I'm some hardcore NFT collector.
          I'm really not. I just like seeing whether I can
          secure the spot. 😂
        


        

          Over the years, that rabbit hole took me through
          a ridiculous number of Web3 projects and ecosystems.
        


        

          Hooked Protocol, Monad, Movement, Story Protocol,
          XION, 0G Labs, ZetaChain, Omni Network, Plume,
          plus a long list of L1s, L2s, and testnets that
          I'll probably suddenly remember at 3 AM.
        


        

          I've tested networks, run nodes, explored infrastructure,
          played around with different hardware, helped people
          in communities, and generally followed whatever looked
          interesting enough to ruin my sleep schedule.
        


        

          Most of it never hits CT. I'm more of a low-key Discord guy.
        


        

          I find a project that interests me, get curious,
          and then go all in — testing, exploring, running things,
          helping out, breaking stuff, fixing it, and figuring out
          how it works.
        


        

          Then I move on to the next rabbit hole.
        


        

          The loop is simple.
        


        

          
            See something interesting. Join the Discord.
            Break it. Fix it. Forget it. Start again.
