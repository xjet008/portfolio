// who.js



Just call me Jet.




  The name was inspired by the GTA: Vice City cheat code
  COMEFLYWITHME, the one that lets you fly.





  There's no deep story or philosophy behind it. I just thought it sounded
  cool, and somehow it became my Web3 identity.





  Honestly, I just prefer it over using my real name here. That's it.


        

  I'm from Attock, Pakistan. If you ever run into me in Web3, I didn't come
  here for the yacht, the Lambo, or the promise of getting rich overnight.
  I came because I was curious. Or maybe just bored. Honestly, I still haven't
  figured out which.





  One random click led to another. Then another. Before I knew it, I had an X
  account, Discord was open 24/7, and I was reading documentation for projects
  I'd only discovered five minutes earlier. Somehow, that became my daily
  routine.





  That's usually how I end up anywhere. I tell myself, "I'll just take a quick
  look," and the next thing I know, I'm buried in Discord threads,
  documentation, and browser tabs I refuse to close.





  Somewhere along the way, I usually break something. Then I spend the next
  hour convincing myself I know how to fix it. Sometimes I do. Most of the
  time, I just create a brand-new problem to solve.





  Ethos has verified that I'm human. My browser history, however, still thinks
  I'm a documentation crawler. Honestly, they're both probably right.





  I don't really have a master plan. I just keep following interesting
  questions. Most of them turn into rabbit holes. A few turn into skills.
  Either way, I rarely regret clicking.



      



      
      


        
// story.md — campaign log / 2019 → 2026

        
fun is the fuel. profit is a rumor.


        
        

          
2019 — curious

  

  Like a lot of people, I found crypto through Bitcoin Cash. I was curious, so
  I bought some, started exploring, and accidentally opened the door to a much
  bigger world.





  I gave trading a shot. It didn't take long to realize I wasn't built for
  staring at candlestick charts all day.





  Please don't send me charts. They work better than sleeping pills.





  What kept me around wasn't the price action. It was everything happening
  behind the scenes. I'd rather spend my time on X, hang out in Discord, fix a
  Linux machine, or read documentation until it finally starts making sense.





  Somewhere along the way, I realized something: the official story and what's
  actually happening aren't always the same thing. Since then, I've been more
  interested in understanding how things really work than simply taking them at
  face value.


        


        
        

          
2020 — how blocks get made



  Curiosity eventually led me to mining.





  Monero. Cardano. A few others.





  I wanted to understand how blocks were actually produced, so I started
  experimenting with everything I could get my hands on—from old PCs that had
  already lived a full life to dedicated mining hardware.





  My hardware requirements were simple: if it booted, it had a job.
  I called it production-ready. The hardware probably called it abuse.





  Along the way, I learned two things: how block production works... and how
  loud a computer can get before you start wondering whether it's mining crypto
  or preparing for takeoff.


        


        
        

          
2021 — college in one window, linux in the other



  I completed my Intermediate with Computer Science.





  School taught me the fundamentals. The internet kept assigning extra
  homework.





  My browser had one tab for college and about twenty for things I'd convinced myself were "more important."





  Somehow, I survived both.


        


        
        

          
2022 — one last game

         
By 2022, I had started drifting away from the internet.


  
Most nights turned into PUBG Mobile.


  
Friends online. Headphones on. Mic full of random yelling.


  
"Last game" became the biggest lie of the year.


  
One match ended, someone said "one more," and somehow we were still playing at 4 AM.


  
Sometimes we'd lose horribly and immediately queue again because obviously the next one would be different.


  
It usually wasn't.


  
I wasn't really doing much online that year. I was mostly just playing, staying up late, and enjoying the nights.


  
Looking back, those were some good nights.


        
        

          
2023–2024 — last game, then one more

          

 The rabbit hole kept getting deeper.





  Web3. Nodes. Testnets. Infrastructure. Ecosystems.





  Somewhere in the middle of all that, I also played way too much PUBG Mobile.





  Late nights. Squad games. Random teammates. Saying "last game" about ten times before finally giving up on the idea.





  There was never just one path. Whatever looked interesting got my attention. More testnets. More infrastructure. More hardware. And, somehow, even more PUBG.


          


  Things still got done. I honestly have no idea how.





  Discord eventually became a second home.





 At one point, I became one of those people who spammed gib wl in Discord, hoping to snag an NFT whitelist or a guaranteed spot.





  Not because I'm a hardcore collector. I'm really not.





  I just liked seeing if I could get the spot. The NFT was optional. Winning the internet lottery was the fun part.


            Priorities.
          

        


        
        

          
2025 — too many tabs
 


  One rabbit hole wasn't enough. So I collected ecosystems instead.





  Monad. Hooked Protocol. Gaia. Node Ops. Movement. Story Protocol. XION. 0G Labs. ZetaChain. Omni Network. Plume. Arkham. Magic Square. bla bla bla.....





  That's the list I can remember during the day. The missing names usually return around 3 AM, right when I'm trying to sleep.





  I tested networks, ran nodes, experimented with hardware, explored infrastructure, and helped people in communities. Basically, if something looked interesting, I convinced myself it was worth losing sleep over.





  Most of it never made it to Crypto Twitter. I'm more of a Discord guy. Less posting, more breaking things to see how they work.





  My workflow isn't exactly complicated.





  Find something interesting. Get curious. Fall down the rabbit hole.





  Test it. Run it. Break it. Fix it. Help someone else fix it. Finally understand it... just in time to find another rabbit hole and repeat the entire process.
